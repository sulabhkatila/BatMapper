How It Works

Understanding the physics of sound and motion to map the real world.

📱

The Hardware Toolkit

Phones have built-in speakers and microphones. The bottom mic captures near-field low frequencies (voice), while the top mic can capture distant, high-frequency sounds. We also use IMU sensors—the Accelerometer (measuring acceleration) and Gyroscope (measuring rotation)—to track exactly how the phone moves in space.

🌊

The Physics of Sound

Sound is a wave. The amplitude determines loudness, and frequency determines pitch. When sound bounces off a surface, it creates an Echo. Natural sounds are complex (many pure tones combined). We use a magical gate called a Butterworth Band-Pass Filter to block all noise and let through only the specific frequencies we care about.

🚶

Dead Reckoning

To map a room, we need to know exactly where we are. If you start at (0, 0), we use the IMU sensors to calculate your new position after every movement. By continually updating your position relative to the last known point, we can accurately track your path through the room.

🦇

Emitting & Finding Echoes

We emit short, high-pitched "chirps" (8-16kHz) followed by silence so echoes don't overlap. The microphone continuously records the incoming sound. By comparing the received signal with the original chirp (cross-correlation), we find moments where they match strongly—these peaks are our echoes.

🗺️

Building the Map

A single echo isn't trustworthy. We collect many measurements over time (probabilistic evidence accumulation) to increase our confidence in a wall's location. Then, we use recursive outlier removal to repeatedly throw away echoes that don't fit the room's geometry, leaving behind a crisp, 2D floor plan.

Technical Architecture

Built with a modular architecture in Swift, separating UI, orchestration, audio processing, and motion tracking.

1

Orchestration (BatMapperViewModel)

The central brain running on the MainActor. Orchestrates all subsystems with a high-frequency (20Hz) refresh timer to keep the SwiftUI interface smooth and reactive.

2

Acoustic Subsystem

ChirpEmitter loops high-frequency chirps using AVAudioPlayer. AudioRecorder taps the microphone at 48kHz, sending audio buffers to a background thread.

3

Signal Processing

Applies a Butterworth filter, performs cross-correlation to match chirps, uses Gaussian smoothing to find peaks, and calculates distances based on the speed of sound.

4

Motion & Map Generation

Leverages CMMotionManager for orientation. Interleaves user steps with wall coordinates to map 2D spaces, and even detects open doors from sudden depth increases.

Privacy First

All computation is done completely locally on your device. The app uses your microphone and speaker to generate map points in real-time, and no data ever leaves your phone.
